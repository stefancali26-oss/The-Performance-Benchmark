HOW TO USE THIS PROGRAM

Full 18 weeks: Run all three phases back to back. This is the complete system — strength into power into full performance. Best done in an off-season or pre-season block.

Strength only (Weeks 1–6): A standalone 4-day strength block. Works alongside your sport training. Focus: building raw strength across all major movement patterns.

Strength & Power (Weeks 7–12): A standalone 4-day block. Requires a solid strength base (or completion of Phase 1). Focus: converting strength into explosiveness through contrast and Olympic lifting.

Full Performance (Weeks 13–18): A 6-day block including running, speed, and injury prevention. Designed for athletes in pre-season or preparing to return to competition. Requires your 2km time trial result to calculate MAS.

WHAT YOU NEED

  • Commercial gym access — barbell, cables, GHD, smith machine, cable machines
  • A track or open space — for Phase 3 running sessions
  • A GPS watch or phone — to measure running distances and speeds
  • Your 1RM on key lifts — see the 1RM Testing Guide before starting Phase 3
  • A 2km time trial result — to calculate your MAS before Phase 3

REST PERIODS

Rest as needed between sets. On main compound lifts (squat, deadlift, bench, seal row) this typically means 2–4 minutes as loads increase across the program. On accessories and isolation work, 60–90 seconds is sufficient. On power and plyometric work, full recovery between efforts — quality over fatigue. Running intervals have rest periods prescribed in the session itself.

DELOAD PROTOCOL

If you miss a full week, or finish a week feeling run-down, do not just continue. Drop volume by 40% and intensity by 10–15% for one week before resuming. This means fewer sets, not fewer exercises.

Signs you need a deload: persistent joint soreness (not muscle soreness), performance dropping across multiple sessions, sleep quality declining, motivation hitting a wall.

Week 12 is a built-in test/reset week. If you're running the full 18 weeks, this is your natural deload point before Phase 3 ramps up in volume and frequency.

Before You Start Phase 3

1RM
TESTING GUIDE

Phase 3 uses percentage-based loading on the main lifts. You need accurate 1RM numbers to make those percentages mean something. A bad 1RM test means every percentage in the program is wrong. Do this properly once, and the whole program runs as designed.

HOW TO TEST YOUR 1RM

Test each lift on a separate day, or pair upper and lower on the same day with adequate rest. Never test on back-to-back days. Don't test if you're sore, sleep-deprived, or coming off a hard week.

StepLoadRepsRest
Warm-up 1~40% estimated 1RM102 min
Warm-up 2~60%53 min
Warm-up 3~75%33 min
Warm-up 4~85%24 min
Attempt 1~92–95%14 min
Attempt 2~97–100%15 min
Attempt 3Max if Attempt 2 was solid15 min

You get a maximum of 3 max attempts. If Attempt 2 felt like a grind, that's your 1RM — don't go again.

LIFTS TO TEST

  • BB Back Squat — test first, when you're freshest
  • Trap Bar Deadlift — test on a separate lower day
  • BB Bench Press — upper body, pair with seal row day
  • BB Seal Row — test after bench, adequate rest between

ESTIMATING FROM A SET

If you can't max test, use the Epley formula: 1RM = Weight × (1 + Reps ÷ 30)

Example: 100kg for 5 reps = 100 × (1 + 5/30) = 100 × 1.167 = 116.7kg estimated 1RM

This is less accurate above 6 reps. Keep rep sets to 5 or below for best estimates.

RECORDING YOUR NUMBERS

Log your 1RMs in the weight tracking fields on Week 13 Day 1 and Day 4. The progression indicators will then show you how your working weights compare across weeks.

Reference

EXERCISE
GLOSSARY

Every exercise in the program, with a description and a direct YouTube link. Abbreviations used throughout: BB = Barbell · DB = Dumbbell · KB = Kettlebell · SL = Single Leg · SA = Single Arm · e/s = each side · BW = Bodyweight

BB Back Squat
Barbell on upper back, squat to parallel or below. Primary lower body compound. Control the descent, drive through the floor on the way up.
▶ Watch
BB RDL
Romanian Deadlift. Hip hinge movement, bar stays close to legs, slight knee bend. Feel the hamstring load at the bottom before driving hips forward.
▶ Watch
BB Deadlift
Conventional deadlift from the floor. Neutral spine, bar over mid-foot, drive the floor away. Full hip extension at lockout.
▶ Watch
Trap Bar Deadlift
Deadlift performed inside a hex/trap bar. More quad-dominant than conventional. More forgiving on lower back. Great for athletic development.
▶ Watch
SL Leg Press
Single leg press on the leg press machine. One foot on the platform, press to full extension without locking the knee. Controls any strength asymmetry between legs.
▶ Watch
BB Hip Thrust
Shoulders on a bench, bar across hip crease, drive hips to full extension. Squeeze glutes at the top. Primary glute development exercise.
▶ Watch
BB Split Squat (concentric only)
From the bottom position of a split squat, drive up explosively. Step or drop back to reset, no eccentric phase. Used for power development — the focus is the upward drive only.
▶ Watch
DB Split Squat
Static lunge position with dumbbells. Front foot flat, back knee drops toward the floor. More stable than a walking lunge — focus on depth and control.
▶ Watch
DB Reverse Lunge
Step back into a lunge with dumbbells. Less knee stress than a forward lunge. Drive through the front heel to return to standing.
▶ Watch
Leg Extension
Machine exercise isolating the quadriceps. Control the lowering phase. Keep your back against the pad throughout. Single leg version: one leg at a time.
▶ Watch
Hamstring Curl
Lying hamstring curl machine. Curl heels toward glutes, control the return. Single leg version trains each hamstring independently.
▶ Watch
GHD Back Extension
On the GHD machine, lower the torso then drive up to parallel using glutes and hamstrings. Do not hyperextend. Can be loaded with a plate held to chest.
▶ Watch
Nordic Eccentric
Kneeling with feet anchored, lower your body toward the floor as slowly as possible using hamstring control. The most effective hamstring injury prevention exercise in the evidence base.
▶ Watch
Dynamic Copenhagen
Side plank with top leg on a bench, bottom leg raises to meet it. Trains hip adductors dynamically. Key groin and pelvic injury prevention movement.
▶ Watch
BB Step Up to Hip Flex
Step up onto a box with a barbell, drive the trailing knee up to hip height at the top. Athletic power movement training single-leg drive and hip flexion under load.
▶ Watch
BB Cleans
Power clean — bar from floor to front rack in one explosive pull. Full triple extension (ankle, knee, hip) at the top. Primary Olympic lifting movement for athletic power.
▶ Watch
Seated Calf Raise
On the seated calf raise machine, full range of motion — deep stretch at the bottom, full contraction at the top. Targets the soleus (deeper calf muscle).
▶ Watch
Smith Machine Calf Raise
Standing calf raise using the Smith machine for load. Targets the gastrocnemius (outer calf). Full range — heel below platform level at the bottom.
▶ Watch
BB Bench Press
Flat barbell bench press. Bar touches chest, press to full extension. Elbows at roughly 45–75 degrees to torso. Feet flat on floor, slight arch in lower back.
▶ Watch
Incline DB Bench Press
Dumbbell press on a 30–45 degree incline bench. Greater upper chest emphasis than flat pressing. Control the lowering phase.
▶ Watch
Smith Machine Chest Press Throw
Explosive press on the Smith machine — push the bar as fast as possible to mimic a throw. Release briefly at the top. Light load, maximum intent. Power exercise.
▶ Watch
BB Push Press
Barbell overhead press with a leg drive initiation. Slight dip and drive through legs, then press overhead. More load than a strict press — develops full body power transfer.
▶ Watch
Pull Up
Overhand grip pull-up. Full hang at bottom, chin over bar at top. Can be loaded with a dipping belt. Do not kip.
▶ Watch
Chin Up
Underhand (supinated) grip pull-up. Greater bicep involvement than pull-ups. Same range — full hang to chin over bar.
▶ Watch
BB Seal Row
Barbell row lying chest-down on a bench elevated on blocks. Removes all body English — pure upper back and lat effort. Row bar to chest, control the lowering.
▶ Watch
Cable Row
Seated cable row with a neutral grip attachment. Drive elbows back, squeeze shoulder blades. Do not round the back or use momentum.
▶ Watch
Lat Pull Down
Cable lat pulldown, wide grip. Pull bar to upper chest, driving elbows down and back. Control the return to a full overhead stretch. Single arm version: SA lat pulldown.
▶ Watch
SA DB Bent Over Row
Single arm dumbbell row with one hand and knee on a bench. Row dumbbell to hip level, elbow close to body. Full range — full stretch at bottom, full contraction at top.
▶ Watch
KB Gorilla Row
Two kettlebells on the floor, hip hinge position, alternate rowing each side while the other hand stabilises on the bell. Core anti-rotation demand plus upper back work.
▶ Watch
Explosive BW Inverted Row
Bodyweight inverted row performed with maximum intent — pull explosively, release briefly at the top. Power exercise for the upper back and pulling patterns.
▶ Watch
Dips
Parallel bar dips. Slight forward lean for chest emphasis, more upright for tricep emphasis. Add weight with a dipping belt as prescribed. Full range — upper arms parallel at bottom.
▶ Watch
Lateral DB Shoulder Raises
Dumbbell lateral raises — arms to shoulder height, slight elbow bend, control the lowering. Targets the medial deltoid.
▶ Watch
Reverse DB Shoulder Raises
Bent over dumbbell reverse fly. Arms out to sides, elbows soft. Targets rear deltoids. Keep the torso parallel to the floor.
▶ Watch
Cable Woodchop
Diagonal cable chop from high to low (or low to high). Rotational core power. Keep arms relatively straight, the movement comes from the hips and trunk.
▶ Watch
Cable Pallof Press
Anti-rotation press. Cable at chest height, press straight out and hold briefly, return. The resistance is sideways — resist rotation throughout. Core stability exercise.
▶ Watch
GHD Sit Up
Full range sit-up on the GHD machine. Arms overhead at the bottom, sit all the way up. Significant hip flexor involvement. Progress cautiously if new to this movement.
▶ Watch
GHD Leg Raise
On the GHD, body inverted, raise legs from hanging to parallel. Lower controlled. Strong hip flexor and lower abdominal demand.
▶ Watch
Hanging Leg/Knee Raises
From a dead hang on a pull-up bar, raise legs (straight) or knees (bent) to hip height or above. Control the lowering. No swinging.
▶ Watch
Heavy Russian Twists
Seated, feet off ground, rotate a weight plate or medicine ball side to side. Load should be heavy enough to challenge rotation — not a light aerobic exercise.
▶ Watch
Dragon Flag
Lying on a bench, hold behind your head, raise your entire body off the bench and lower under control. One of the most demanding anti-extension core exercises.
▶ Watch
Ab Roll Out
Kneeling or standing with an ab wheel or barbell, roll forward under control, return without letting the hips sag. Anti-extension core training.
▶ Watch
Cable Abduction / Adduction
Standing cable hip abduction (leg out to side) or adduction (leg across body). Trains the hip stabilisers and groin. Control both directions of movement.
▶ Watch
Suitcase Carry
Heavy dumbbell or kettlebell in one hand, walk the prescribed distance. Resist lateral trunk lean — stay tall. Trains core stability, grip, and loaded gait patterns.
▶ Watch
Landmine Woodchop / Lateral Lunge
Barbell anchored in a landmine attachment. Woodchop: rotate the bar diagonally across the body. Lateral lunge: step to the side with the bar for a loaded lateral pattern.
▶ Watch
Box Jump
Jump onto a box, land softly with knees tracking over toes. Step down — do not jump down. Focus is on explosive intent on the jump, not getting on a high box.
▶ Watch
Broad Jump
Standing long jump — maximum horizontal distance. Land with both feet, absorb through hips and knees. Measures horizontal power output.
▶ Watch
Banded CMJ
Countermovement jump with a resistance band added for accommodating resistance. Increases rate of force development demand. Jump as high as possible.
▶ Watch
MB Chest Throw
Medicine ball thrown from chest against a wall or to a partner. Maximum explosive intent. Not a slow controlled push — throw it as hard as possible.
▶ Watch
Plyo Push Up
Explosive push-up where hands leave the floor at the top. Can be with or without a clap. Maximum speed on the push phase.
▶ Watch
DB French Press
Lying or seated overhead tricep extension with a dumbbell or EZY bar. Lower the weight behind the head, extend to lockout. Keep elbows pointed forward.
▶ Watch
Cable Tricep Extension / Push Down
Cable pushdown for triceps. Keep elbows pinned to sides, extend fully, control the return. Rope or bar attachment — both work.
▶ Watch
EZY Bar Curl / BB Biceps Curl
Bicep curl with an EZY bar or straight barbell. Elbows at sides, curl to full contraction, lower with control. Do not swing.
▶ Watch
Zottman Curl
Curl up with a supinated (underhand) grip, rotate to a pronated (overhand) grip at the top, lower slowly with the overhand grip. Trains biceps and forearm extensors together.
▶ Watch
DB / Smith Machine JM Press
Hybrid between a bench press and skull crusher. Bar or dumbbells lower toward the chin/throat with elbows flared slightly. Excellent tricep mass builder with less elbow stress.
▶ Watch
MAS Running (% MAS)
Running at a set percentage of your Maximum Aerobic Speed. Calculate your MAS from the 2km time trial. All running distances in Phase 3 are based on your individual MAS — use the calculator in the Phase 3 intro.
▶ Watch
Modified Monoghetti
Interval running protocol where work and rest periods decrease progressively. Each round gets shorter. The structure is 90/90 × reps · 60/60 × reps · 30/30 × reps — the numbers are seconds of work / seconds of rest.
▶ Watch
TABATA
20 seconds of maximum effort work, 10 seconds of rest, repeated for 8 rounds. Total 4 minutes. In this program applied to running or conditioning exercises. True Tabata requires maximum output on every rep.

Running intensity progresses week by week from 100% MAS up to 130% MAS. Nordic eccentrics progress on Speed Days only, from 3×1 to weighted eccentrics in the final two weeks.
